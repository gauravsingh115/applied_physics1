circular motion — angular velocity & acceleration

Spin rate changing? That's angular acceleration

ω tells you how fast something is rotating right now. Angular acceleration, α, tells you how quickly that spin rate itself is changing — speeding up, slowing down, same idea as ordinary acceleration, just for angles. And because v = rω, a change in ω shows up as an actual, physical tangential acceleration for any point away from the center.

Angular velocity, ω

The instantaneous rate of change of angular position. Points along the rotation axis in three dimensions, but for a single plane of rotation, its sign alone tells you the direction.

Angular acceleration, α

The rate of change of ω. Constant α gives the same kind of kinematics you already know, just relabelled.

ω = ω₀ + αt    θ = ω₀t + ½αt²

Tangential acceleration, a_t

The physical, linear acceleration of a point at radius r, caused purely by the spin rate changing — distinct from the centripetal acceleration that exists even at constant ω.

a_t = rα

Problem 1 — concept check

A disk's angular speed increases at a constant rate as it spins. What does that imply about a point on its rim, at fixed radius r?

Problem 2

A turbine spins up from rest to 150 rad/s in 6 s, at a constant angular acceleration. Find α.

rad/s²
Show solution
α = (ω − ω₀)/t = (150 − 0)/6 = 25 rad/s².

Problem 3

A point at radius 0.8 m on that turbine — find its tangential acceleration during spin-up.

m/s²
Show solution
a_t = rα = 0.8 × 25 = 20 m/s².

Problem 4

Using the same turbine (ω₀ = 0, α = 25 rad/s²), find the angular displacement after 6 s, in both radians and revolutions.

rad
Show solution
θ = ω₀t + ½αt² = 0 + 0.5 × 25 × 36 = 450 rad, which is 450 / 2π ≈ 71.6 revolutions.
