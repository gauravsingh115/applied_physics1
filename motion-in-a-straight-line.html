kinematics — motion in a straight line

Everything happens on one number line

Restrict motion to a single line and two ideas take over: where you are relative to a fixed origin, and how fast you're closing in on — or pulling away from — someone else on that same line. Set two travelers moving and watch whether, and when, their paths actually cross.

Position on a line

Pick an origin and a positive direction. Every position is then just one signed number — negative means on the other side of the origin, not "less distance."

x(t) = x₀ + vt

Relative velocity

How fast the gap between two travelers is closing, seen from either one. It's just the difference of their velocities — and if it's zero, the gap never changes at all.

v(A rel. to B) = vA − vB

Practice

Work these by hand, then check yourself.

Problem 1 — concept check

Two cars travel in the same direction on a straight highway. Car A moves at 25 m/s, and Car B — ahead of it — moves at 18 m/s. What is the velocity of car A relative to car B?

Problem 2

Two trains are 120 km apart on the same straight track, moving toward each other — one at 60 km/h, the other at 40 km/h. How long until they meet?

h
Show solution
Moving toward each other, their closing speed adds: 60 + 40 = 100 km/h. Time to close a 120 km gap: 120 ⁄ 100 = 1.2 h.

Problem 3

Particle A starts at x = −5 m moving at +3 m/s. Particle B starts at x = +10 m moving at −2 m/s. Find the time and position at which they meet.

s
m
Show solution
Set positions equal: −5 + 3t = 10 − 2t, so 5t = 15, giving t = 3 s. Substituting back: x = −5 + 3(3) = 4 m.

Problem 4

Two cyclists ride in the same direction, both at 5 m/s, one always 8 m ahead of the other. What is their relative velocity, and does the 8 m gap ever change?

m/s
Show solution
Relative velocity is 5 − 5 = 0 m/s. With zero relative velocity the gap never changes — the two cyclists stay exactly 8 m apart forever, even though both keep moving.
