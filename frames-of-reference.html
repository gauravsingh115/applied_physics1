dynamics — frames of reference

Motion only makes sense once you say who's watching

"Moving" is meaningless on its own — moving compared to what? A reference frame is the vantage point you measure position, velocity, and acceleration from. Change the frame and the numbers change with it, even though nothing about the actual object has. Watch a ball roll inside a train, from two different seats.

Reference frame

A coordinate system, plus a clock, that an observer measures motion from. Every velocity and acceleration you've ever calculated was secretly measured relative to some frame.

Inertial frame

A frame that isn't accelerating. Newton's laws work in their simple, familiar form here: no force, no acceleration.

Non-inertial frame

A frame that is accelerating — speeding up, slowing down, or turning. Objects here appear to accelerate even when no real force is acting on them.

Problem 2

A passenger walks at 5 km/h toward the front of a train that is moving at 60 km/h, both in the same direction. Find the passenger's speed relative to the ground.

km/h
Show solution
Velocities in the same direction simply add when you switch from the train's frame to the ground's frame: 60 + 5 = 65 km/h.

Problem 3

A boat can move at 8 m/s relative to the water. It heads upstream against a river flowing at 3 m/s. Find the boat's speed relative to the ground (the riverbank).

m/s
Show solution
Going upstream, the current works against the boat, so the velocities subtract: 8 − 3 = 5 m/s relative to the bank.

Problem 4

A train accelerates at 2 m/s². A ball sits on its frictionless floor, initially at rest relative to the train. Find the ball's acceleration as measured from the ground frame, and its apparent acceleration as measured from the train's frame (take acceleration in the train's direction of travel as positive).

m/s²
m/s²
Show solution
No real horizontal force acts on the ball, so in the ground (inertial) frame its acceleration is 0 m/s² — it just stays put while the train moves out from under it. Watched from inside the accelerating train, though, the ball appears to slide backward with acceleration −2 m/s², exactly opposite the train's own acceleration. That apparent acceleration, with nothing real causing it, is the seed of the next topic: pseudo forces.
