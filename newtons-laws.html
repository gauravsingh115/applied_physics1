dynamics — newton's laws of motion

Three rules that explain almost everything that moves

Newton's three laws connect force to motion. The first says what happens with no net force at all, the second says exactly how much a given net force changes things, and the third says forces never show up alone — they arrive in pairs. Push a cart below and watch all three show up in one simulator.

First law inertia

An object at rest stays at rest, and an object in motion keeps moving at constant velocity, unless a net external force acts on it.

Second law F = ma

Net force causes acceleration. More force means more acceleration; more mass means less, for the same force.

F_net = m·a

Third law action & reaction

Whenever object A pushes on object B, B pushes back on A with equal magnitude, opposite direction — always as a pair, never alone.

F_A→B = −F_B→A

The push-off — third law in action

Two skaters on frictionless ice push apart with equal and opposite forces. Set both masses, then push.

skater A's speed = 0.00 m/s     skater B's speed = 0.00 m/s

Both skaters feel the exact same push force, for the exact same instant — but the lighter one ends up moving faster, because the same force divided by a smaller mass gives more acceleration.

Straight from the second law: F = ma = 1000 × 2.5 = 2500 N.

Problem 3

A 800 kg drone's rotors provide 12000 N of upward thrust. Find the net upward force after accounting for its weight, and the resulting upward acceleration.

m/s²
Show solution
Weight pulls down with mg = 800 × 9.8 = 7840 N. Net upward force is thrust minus weight: 12000 − 7840 = 4160 N. Acceleration follows from the second law: a = 4160 / 800 = 5.2 m/s², upward.

Problem 4

A 70 kg astronaut pushes off a 4000 kg capsule with a force of 210 N. Find the magnitude of the astronaut's acceleration and the capsule's acceleration. (By the third law, both feel the same 210 N, in opposite directions.)

m/s²
m/s²
Show solution
Same force, different mass, different acceleration: astronaut a = 210 / 70 = 3 m/s²; capsule a = 210 / 4000 = 0.0525 m/s². The much heavier capsule barely notices the push — this is why astronauts can drift away from a station with a light tap.
