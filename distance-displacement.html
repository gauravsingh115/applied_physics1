kinematics — distance & displacement

How far you went vs. where you ended up

Walk a path on the grid below. One number tracks every step your feet actually took. The other only cares about the straight line from where you started to where you are now. They are rarely the same number.

Distance scalar

The total length of the path actually travelled, measuring every twist and backtrack. It only ever increases as you move — it has a size, but no direction.

Displacement vector

The straight-line change from start to end position — a single arrow with a magnitude and a direction. It can shrink, vanish, or even point backward as you keep walking.

Problem 2

An athlete runs one full lap around a circular track of radius 63 m and stops back at the starting line. Find the distance covered and the magnitude of the displacement.

Distance is the full circumference: 2πr = 2π(63) ≈ 395.8 m. Since the athlete returns exactly to the start, the displacement is 0 m — no matter how long the run, start and end coincide.

Problem 3

A hiker walks 6 km due east, then 8 km due north. Find the total distance walked and the magnitude of the displacement.

km
km
Show solution
Distance is just the sum of the legs: 6 + 8 = 14 km. Displacement is the straight line from start to end, found with Pythagoras: √(6² + 8²) = √100 = 10 km, directed roughly 53.1° north of east.

Problem 4

A train moves 240 m forward along a straight track, then reverses and moves 90 m back. Taking the forward direction as positive, find the distance travelled and the displacement.

m
m
Show solution
Distance adds both legs regardless of direction: 240 + 90 = 330 m. Displacement is the net change in position: 240 − 90 = +150 m (forward). On a straight line, distance and displacement can differ even without ever leaving the line.
