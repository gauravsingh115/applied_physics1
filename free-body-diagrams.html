dynamics — free body diagrams

One object, every real force, nothing else

A free body diagram strips a problem down to its essentials: pick one object, ignore everything it's touching or connected to, and draw every real external force acting on it as an arrow from its centre. No motion, no speed, no forces it exerts on other things — just the forces it feels. Get the diagram right and the equations practically write themselves.

Isolate the object

Mentally cut the object free from the rope, the table, the incline — everything. Replace each connection with the force it was exerting.

Only forces on it

Draw only forces acting on the object, never forces it exerts on something else — those belong on a different diagram.

Arrows from the centre

Every force is drawn as an arrow starting at the object's centre, with length roughly matching its relative size, and pointing the right way.

Problem 2

A 5 kg block sits on a frictionless incline tilted at 30°. Find the component of gravity pulling it down the slope, and the normal force the incline exerts on it.

N
N
Show solution
Weight is mg = 5 × 9.8 = 49 N, straight down. Split it into components along and perpendicular to the incline: along the slope, mg sin30° = 49 × 0.5 = 24.5 N; perpendicular to it, mg cos30° = 49 × 0.866 ≈ 42.4 N, which the normal force must exactly balance since the block doesn't lift off or sink into the ramp.

Problem 3

Two blocks, 4 kg and 6 kg, hang from a light string over a frictionless pulley (an Atwood machine). Find the acceleration of the system and the tension in the string.

m/s²
N
Show solution
Draw a separate FBD for each block: weight down, tension up, same tension on both since the string and pulley are ideal. Writing Newton's second law for each and combining gives a = (m₂ − m₁)g / (m₁ + m₂) = (6 − 4)(9.8) / 10 = 1.96 m/s². The tension follows from either block's equation: T = m₁(g + a) = 4 × 11.76 = 47.04 N — and checking with the heavier block, T = m₂(g − a) = 6 × 7.84 = 47.04 N too.

Problem 4

A 10 kg box is pushed along a floor by a 50 N force applied at 20° above the horizontal. Find the normal force the floor exerts on the box, accounting for the upward pull of the applied force's vertical component.

N
Show solution
The free body diagram needs four forces: weight down, normal up, the applied push (angled), and friction. Only the vertical forces matter here. Weight is mg = 98 N down; the applied force's vertical component is F sin20° = 50 × 0.342 ≈ 17.1 N, pulling up. Since the box doesn't accelerate vertically, N = mg − F sin20° = 98 − 17.1 ≈ 80.9 N — noticeably less than the box's weight alone.
