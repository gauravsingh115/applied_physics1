kinematics — equations of motion

Five quantities, two equations, endless problems

Whenever acceleration is constant, initial velocity, final velocity, acceleration, time, and displacement are all locked together. Know any three, and the other two are no longer a mystery — just algebra. Fill in three values below and let the solver find the rest.

Velocity is a straight line

With steady acceleration, v-t is a straight line: start height u, slope a.

v = u + at

Displacement is the area

The area under that line splits into a rectangle (ut) and a triangle (½at²).

s = ut + ½at²

Eliminate time entirely

Combine the two equations above and t cancels out completely.

v² = u² + 2as

Problem 1

A car moving at 5 m/s accelerates at 2 m/s² for 4 s. Find its final velocity and the distance it covers.

m/s
m
Show solution
v = u + at = 5 + (2)(4) = 13 m/s. s = ut + ½at² = 5(4) + ½(2)(16) = 20 + 16 = 36 m.

Problem 2

Something is dropped so it moves downward at 4 m/s already, then accelerates downward at 10 m/s². How far does it fall in the next 3 s?

m
Show solution
s = ut + ½at² = 4(3) + ½(10)(9) = 12 + 45 = 57 m.

Problem 3

A car moving at 20 m/s brakes, decelerating at 5 m/s². How far does it travel before stopping?

m
Show solution
No time is given, so eliminate it: v² = u² + 2as → 0 = 400 + 2(−5)s → s = 40 m.

Problem 4 — two answers

A ball is thrown straight up at 20 m/s, with gravity giving it −10 m/s². At what time(s) is it at a height of 15 m?

s
s
Show solution
15 = 20t − 5t² rearranges to 5t² − 20t + 15 = 0, or t² − 4t + 3 = 0, giving t = 1 s and t = 3 s — once on the way up, once on the way down.
