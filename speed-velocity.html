kinematics — speed & velocity

How fast, and how fast in a straight line home

Speed is what your legs feel — a single number for how quickly ground passes underfoot. Velocity is what a compass would show — that same rate, but pointing somewhere. Walk the path below and watch the two readings agree moment to moment, yet drift apart the moment you average.

Speed scalar

The rate at which distance is covered. Always positive, no direction attached — a speedometer needle only ever points forward.

speed = distance ⁄ time

Velocity vector

The rate at which displacement changes — a magnitude and a direction together. Turn a corner at the same speed and velocity still changes, because the direction did.

velocity = displacement ⁄ time

Problem 1 — concept check

A car drives once around a circular track at a constant instantaneous speed of 20 m/s and stops exactly where it started. What are the average speed and average velocity for the lap?

Problem 2

A cyclist covers 120 km of winding road in 4 hours, including stops. Find the average speed for the trip.

km/h
Show solution
Average speed only needs total distance and total time, regardless of how winding the road was: 120 km ÷ 4 h = 30 km/h.

Problem 3

A hiker walks 4 km due east in 1 hour, then 3 km due north in 1 more hour. Find the average speed and the magnitude of the average velocity for the full 2-hour walk.

km/h
km/h
Show solution
Total distance is 4 + 3 = 7 km over 2 h, so average speed = 3.5 km/h. Displacement is the straight line from start to end: √(4² + 3²) = 5 km, so average velocity = 5 ⁄ 2 = 2.5 km/h — noticeably less than the average speed, even though the hiker never doubled back.

Problem 4

A boat's velocity has components vx = 3 m/s (east) and vy = 4 m/s (north). Find the boat's speed and its direction, measured as an angle north of east.

m/s
°
Show solution
Speed is the magnitude of the velocity vector: √(3² + 4²) = 5 m/s. Direction comes from tan⁻¹(vy ⁄ vx) = tan⁻¹(4/3) ≈ 53.1° north of east.
