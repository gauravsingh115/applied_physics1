circular motion — centripetal & centrifugal

Constant speed, constantly accelerating

A ball on a string, moving in a perfect circle at perfectly constant speed, is still accelerating — because its velocity's direction never stops changing. That acceleration always points straight at the center, and it's called centripetal ("center-seeking"). The outward push you feel on a tight turn isn't a real force pulling you outward — it's your own inertia, and it only appears if you insist on describing things from inside the spinning frame.

Centripetal acceleration

Required for any object moving in a circle, even at constant speed, because velocity's direction is always changing. Always points toward the center.

a_c = v²/r = ω²r

Centripetal force

Not a new, separate kind of force — just the name for whichever real force (tension, gravity, friction, normal force) supplies the net inward pull.

F_c = ma_c = mω²r

Centrifugal "force"

A pseudo-force, felt only by an observer rotating along with the object. It points outward and has the same magnitude as the real centripetal force.

Problem 2

A car rounds a curve of radius 50 m at a constant 20 m/s. Find its centripetal acceleration.

m/s²
Show solution
a_c = v²/r = 400/50 = 8 m/s².

Problem 3

That car has a mass of 1200 kg. Find the centripetal force needed to hold it on the curve (in reality, supplied by friction with the road).

N
Show solution
F_c = ma_c = 1200 × 8 = 9600 N.

Problem 4

A 0.5 kg ball on a 0.8 m string moves at angular speed ω = 6 rad/s. Find the tension in the string.

N
Show solution
T = mω²r = 0.5 × 36 × 0.8 = 14.4 N.
