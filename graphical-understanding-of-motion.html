kinematics — graphical understanding of motion

Three graphs, one motion

Position, velocity, and acceleration are the same story told three times. Each graph's slope is the next graph's height, and each graph's area is the next graph's change. Scrub through a motion below and watch all three move together.

Slope tells you the next graph up

The steeper a curve, the faster the quantity below it is changing. A tangent line's slope is the reading on the graph one level up.

slope of x-t → v
slope of v-t → a

Area tells you the next graph down

The space between a curve and the zero line, added up over time, gives the total change in the quantity one level below.

area under v-t → Δx
area under a-t → Δv

Problem 2

On a position-time graph, an object is at x = 4 m when t = 2 s, and at x = 20 m when t = 6 s. Find its average velocity over that interval.

m/s
Show solution
Average velocity is the slope of the straight line (secant) connecting the two points: (20 − 4) ⁄ (6 − 2) = 4 m/s.

Problem 3

A velocity-time graph is a horizontal line at 5 m/s from t = 0 to t = 8 s. Use the area under the graph to find the displacement over that time.

m
Show solution
The shaded region is a rectangle: 5 m/s × 8 s = 40 m.

Problem 4

An object starts at rest and its velocity rises in a straight line to 12 m/s after 6 s of constant acceleration. Use the area under the v-t graph to find the displacement.

m
Show solution
The shaded region is a triangle, not a rectangle, since velocity isn't constant: ½ × 6 s × 12 m/s = 36 m.
