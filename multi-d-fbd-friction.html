dynamics — multi-dimensional free body diagrams with friction

When the push doesn't line up with the slope

A flat free body diagram is easy once every force lines up with your axes. Real problems are rarely that polite — a rope pulls at some angle above a ramp, and suddenly weight, normal force, tension, and friction are all pointing in different directions. The fix is the same trick every time: pick axes along and perpendicular to the surface, decompose everything that doesn't already line up, and let friction and the normal force fall out of whichever equation they belong to.

Choose helpful axes

On an incline, axes along and perpendicular to the surface are almost always easier than horizontal/vertical — normal force and friction already line up with them.

Decompose what's left

Weight, and any applied force at an angle, need to be split into along-slope and perpendicular-to-slope components using sine and cosine of the relevant angles.

Friction follows the surface

Friction always acts along the contact surface, opposing sliding or the tendency to slide — never straight up, down, or sideways unless the surface itself is oriented that way.

Problem 2

A 10 kg block rests on a 25° incline with μ_s = 0.25 and no applied force. Find the normal force, and the minimum μ_s that would actually be needed to keep it from sliding.

N
Show solution
With no applied force, N = mg cosθ = 98 × 0.906 ≈ 88.8 N. To hold the block, static friction would need μ_s ≥ tanθ = tan25° ≈ 0.466 — more than the 0.25 given, so this block actually slides.

Problem 3

A rope pulls a 20 kg block straight up an 18° incline (rope parallel to the surface, φ = 0). μ_s = 0.4. Find the tension needed to put the block exactly on the verge of sliding up.

N
Show solution
With φ = 0, the general formula simplifies to F = mg(sinθ + μ_s cosθ) = 20 × 9.8 × (sin18° + 0.4 cos18°) = 196 × (0.309 + 0.380) ≈ 135.0 N.

Problem 4

A rope at φ = 10° above a 15° incline pulls a 12 kg block, already sliding up, with tension 80 N. μ_k = 0.2. Find the normal force and the block's acceleration.

N
m/s²
Show solution
Normal force: N = mg cosθ − F sinφ = 117.6 × 0.966 − 80 × 0.174 ≈ 113.6 − 13.9 ≈ 99.7 N. Kinetic friction opposes the upward slide, so it points down-slope: f_k = μ_k N ≈ 19.9 N. Along the slope: F cosφ − mg sinθ − f_k = 80 × 0.985 − 117.6 × 0.259 − 19.9 ≈ 78.8 − 30.5 − 19.9 ≈ 28.4 N, so a = 28.4 / 12 ≈ 2.37 m/s² up the slope.
