Relative Motion

Velocity depends on who is measuring it. A train's speed on the ground differs from its speed as seen by someone riding a second train. The rule is vector subtraction: velocity of A relative to B equals vA − vB.

Everything below lives on a single line with a fixed origin and a positive direction (east). A position is just one signed number — negative means on the other side of the origin, not "less distance" — and each train's position at time t is x(t) = x0 + vt.
