dynamics — normal force & coefficient of friction

Tilt the ramp until it slips, and you've just measured μ

The normal force isn't some fixed property of an object — it's whatever a surface needs to supply to stop things from passing through it, and it changes with the situation. Tilt a surface, and the normal force shrinks while a new component of gravity appears along the slope. Push that tilt far enough and you reach the exact angle where friction can no longer hold on — a simple, elegant way to measure the coefficient of friction without ever touching a force sensor.

Normal force

The perpendicular contact force a surface exerts to stop an object pressing into it. It adjusts to whatever equilibrium demands — it is not always equal to weight.

Coefficient of friction

The dimensionless ratio μ = f / N. It describes a pair of surfaces, not a single material — rubber on ice and rubber on asphalt have very different μ.

μ = f / N

Angle of repose

The incline angle at which a resting object is right on the verge of sliding. At that exact angle, gravity's pull along the slope exactly equals the maximum static friction.

tanθ = μ_s

Find the angle of repose

Raise the incline angle slowly. The normal force shrinks, the along-slope pull grows, and friction keeps adjusting to match — until it can't.

Holding. Static friction is easily enough to keep the block in place at this angle.

Problem 2

A 10 kg block sits on a 25° frictionless incline. Find the normal force on it, and the minimum coefficient of static friction that would be needed to keep an identical block from sliding at that angle.

N
Show solution
Normal force is N = mg cos25° = 98 × 0.906 ≈ 88.8 N. To hold the block in place, static friction would need to match gravity's pull along the slope, mg sin25° = 98 × 0.423 ≈ 41.4 N. Since f_s,max = μ_s N, the minimum coefficient is μ_s = tan25° ≈ 0.466.

Problem 3

A block on an adjustable ramp starts sliding right when the ramp reaches 25°. Find μ_s for this block-ramp pair.

Show solution
At the angle of repose, tanθ = μ_s, so μ_s = tan25° ≈ 0.466.

Problem 4

A 5 kg block is already sliding down a 40° incline with μ_k = 0.3. Find its acceleration down the slope.

m/s²
Show solution
Along the slope: a = g(sinθ − μ_k cosθ) = 9.8 × (sin40° − 0.3 × cos40°) = 9.8 × (0.643 − 0.230) = 9.8 × 0.413 ≈ 4.05 m/s², directed down the slope.
